H - The depth rule as the Methods now state it
----------------------------------------------
A landslide whose scarp or source area is delineated as a polygon
distinct from the deposit is deep-seated; one represented by a single
polygon is shallow; and a debris flow is shallow whatever its polygon
structure, because its upslope polygon is a concave erosional and
transport zone and not the scarp of a failure surface.

The submitted text stated the first two clauses and omitted the third.
Run against that text this check flagged 79 debris flows, which were
not defects: the data was right and the description incomplete. The
criterion is encoded here as the Methods now state it, so those records
are no longer findings, and what the check reports is disagreement with
the published criterion rather than with an earlier draft of it.

J - Shape_Area attribute versus computed area
---------------------------------------------
Shape_Area is compared against BOTH the planar grid area of the projected
coordinates and the geodesic area on the GRS80 ellipsoid, because the two
differ here and a GIS can be asked for either. HTRS96 / Croatia TM has its
central meridian at 16.5 deg E with a scale factor of 0.9999, and the
Dubracina valley lies about 145 km west of it, where the grid scale has
grown back above unity: grid areas run about 0.03 % larger than ellipsoidal
ones. A field recomputed with the geodesic option therefore disagrees with
the grid area by that amount without being wrong.

L - Landslides represented by a source polygon alone
----------------------------------------------------
Check F above pairs source and deposit only for landslides ALREADY
represented by two polygons, so it cannot see a landslide whose scarp and
deposit were given different identifiers: each half then looks like a
well-formed single-polygon landslide. The converse test is made here. A
source area is the upper part of a landslide and cannot be a landslide on
its own, with one documented exception: debris flows are mapped by their
detachment and transport zone alone wherever no distinct fan was
deposited, which is why xdf and wxdf appear here legitimately. Any other
class occurring as a lone source polygon is an anomaly, and for each one
the neighbouring deposits of the matching landslide type carrying a
different identifier are listed, because that is the signature of one
landslide split across two identifiers.
